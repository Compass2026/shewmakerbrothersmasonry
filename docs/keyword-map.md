## Method and stated limitations

Seed source: approved service taxonomy crossed with the service-area towns, per the Playbook 3 method note. Category-based keyword-idea endpoints were not used.

#### Limitation: no metro designation

Lake of the Ozarks has no metro or DMA designation in Google Ads location data. City-level pulls at Osage Beach returned volumes of 10 or null across nearly every term, which reflects a town of roughly 5,000 people rather than a data failure. All figures in this document are therefore Missouri state-level and should be read as relative demand between services, not as local absolute demand.

Practical effect: statewide figures overstate terms concentrated in St. Louis and Kansas City (notably tuckpointing) and understate terms concentrated at the Lake (notably seawall and rip rap). Both distortions are noted where they matter below.

#### Limitation: no Search Console history

No existing website and no Search Console property, so there is no first-party query data. The first quarterly review should rebuild this map from scratch once the site has 90 days of history and diff it against this version.

#### Three readings that change the build

Fire pit is a trap. 3,600 searches at a $0.65 CPC is people pricing a portable fire pit at a big-box store, not commissioning a built stone one. Do not build a standalone page against this term; fold fire pits into the outdoor living hub as a section.

Tuckpointing needs discounting. 1,000 volume and $17.58 CPC is real, but Missouri tuckpointing demand sits in St. Louis brick housing stock. The Lake is not that market. Build the page; do not forecast St. Louis numbers from it.

Seawall and rip rap are understated, not unimportant. Statewide figures average a lakefront service across a mostly landlocked state. Sam reports shoreline work as a major revenue line, and the Facebook record supports it. Judge these pages on contracts won, not on ranking volume, and record that here so nobody later reads them as underperforming.

| **Competitor** | **Rating** | **Reviews** | **What they are** |

| Horseshoe Bend Dock & Rip Rap (Lake Ozark) | 5.0 | 11 | Rip rap plus dock work |
| RT Construction / RT Rip Rap (Rocky Mount) | 4.5 | 8 | Rip rap, docks, general construction |

These firms install dumped or placed rock for erosion control, usually attached to a dock business. Shewmaker builds mortared stone seawalls on a concrete footing — a different product, a different price point and a different buyer. They are not direct competitors so much as the commodity alternative.

Positioning consequence: the shoreline page should not fight for rip rap on price. It should convert the searcher who typed "rip rap" and wants to know what else exists. Rip rap protects the shoreline; a stone seawall protects it and looks like part of the property.

### Masonry: effectively uncontested

No masonry contractor at the Lake holds a competitive Google Business Profile. The only masonry listing found near Osage Beach is Smith Mason Contractor, with a single review. The strong masonry profiles in the state — Stone Creek Masonry, JB Masonry — are two hours away in the Kansas City area and are not competitors for this market. Heartland Landscape Group (5.0, 14 reviews, Osage Beach) builds retaining walls, but as part of landscape work.

Nobody owns "masonry contractor Lake of the Ozarks." A complete GBP with real project photography and a review base can take it inside a season.

### Immediate priority: the GBP

Shewmaker currently has no listing, no reviews and no ranking presence. Rock Works has 35 reviews. Every downstream ranking outcome in this map depends on the profile existing first. Creating the profile and generating an initial review base is the highest-leverage task in the engagement and should not wait for the website.

## City tiers

Ten locations in the service area. Tiering follows the Playbook 3 rule: never default to one page per city. Lakefront density and business concentration drive the tiers more than population, because the work is lakefront work.

| **Tier** | **Towns** | **Treatment** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 — build full | Osage Beach, Lake Ozark, Camdenton, Sunrise Beach | Full city pages: local lakefront specifics, Ameren permitting, county detail, project example slots, service links |
| Tier 2 — build lighter | Horseshoe Bend, Gravois Mills, Laurie, Eldon, Versailles | Shorter pages: services offered, communities served, one or two project references |
| Fold in | Hurricane Deck | Unincorporated. Covered as a section on the Sunrise Beach page and on the service-area page, not a standalone page |

Gravois Mills sits in Tier 2 despite its size because it is the base of operations and carries proximity relevance for the GBP.

Horseshoe Bend is Tier 2 rather than Tier 1 despite its lakefront density, because it sits inside Lake Ozark. Two full pages covering the same corridor would compete with each other. The Horseshoe Bend page is built as a supporting area page that links up to Lake Ozark, not as a parallel city page.

Lake Ozark and Horseshoe Bend are the competitive heartland. Horseshoe Bend Dock & Rip Rap, RT Construction and the Rock Works / One Source operation are all based in or adjacent to this corridor, and it holds the densest concentration of high-value lakefront property on the lake. These pages will be contested in a way the Versailles and Eldon pages will not, and they are also where the stone-seawall-versus-rip-rap argument has to be made most clearly.

Ameren permitting is the local content hook for every Tier 1 page. Shoreline work at the Lake of the Ozarks requires Ameren Missouri lakefront permits, and no competitor page explains that process well. It is genuine local content that cannot be templated.
